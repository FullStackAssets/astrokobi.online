When the name Jack Parsons surfaces, most think of rockets, not rituals. In the winter of 1946, the self‑taught chemist who helped found JPL walked into the Mojave Desert with science‑fiction writer L. Ron Hubbard to perform a series of occult ceremonies called the Babalon Working. Their goal was to summon an elemental feminine deity, not to test a new propellant. The episode sits at the crossroads of American rocketry and the occult, reminding us that the people who launch rockets can also be drawn to fringe ideas. Understanding this episode helps us see how cultural currents can shape the narratives around space exploration.

<Callout type="takeaway">Jack Parsons’ occult experiments show that even pioneering scientists can be drawn into fringe beliefs, shaping cultural narratives of space exploration.</Callout>

## What happened

Jack Parsons was born in 1914, became a leading figure in 1930s‑40s rocket development, and conducted the Arroyo Seco experiments on Halloween 1936 that are now recognized as the foundation of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory.

In early 1946 he partnered with L. Ron Hubbard, then a struggling writer, and together they staged the Babalon Working in the Mojave Desert, a sequence of rituals intended to invoke the goddess Babalon and open a doorway for an otherworldly entity.

## Why it matters

The collaboration illustrates how the boundary between scientific ambition and mystical yearning was porous in post‑war America. While the rituals produced no technical advances, they contributed to a mythos that surrounds early rocketry, influencing public perception and later cultural works that blend space and the supernatural. Historians see the episode as a cautionary tale about the personal beliefs of innovators and the way those beliefs can be amplified in popular memory.

## How to think about it

Treat the Babalon Working as a cultural artifact rather than a scientific experiment. When evaluating historical figures, separate documented technical work from personal belief systems, and consider how later storytelling may amplify the latter. Use the episode to discuss the broader theme that innovators operate within the cultural currents of their time, which can include both rational inquiry and mystical curiosity.
