After 17 years of silence, the legendary sci‑fi magazine Starlog is back, debuting its first annual print issue on Nov. 13. The revival coincides with the title’s 50th anniversary, a milestone that underscores its historic role in shaping genre fandom. In an era dominated by digital content, the decision to print a yearly edition signals a growing appetite for tactile, curated media among enthusiasts.

<Callout type="takeaway">Starlog’s return shows that premium print can thrive alongside digital fatigue.</Callout>

## What happened
Starlog ceased publication in 2009 after a 33‑year run, falling victim to the broader decline of specialty print magazines. On Nov. 13, 2026, the brand re‑emerged as an annual print edition—Issue #375—available for pre‑order, with cover art by Armenian illustrator Gabriel Yeganyan. The revival is backed by Fangoria Holdings and io9 co‑founder Annalee Newitz, who now serve as editor‑in‑chief.

The magazine originally launched in 1976, before the release of Star Wars, under editor‑in‑chief Terry O’Quinn. Its early issues featured painted covers such as Jack Thurston’s Star Trek illustration and offered in‑depth interviews, TV guides, movie retrospectives, and VFX features that defined sci‑fi journalism for three decades.

## Why it matters
Starlog’s comeback illustrates a broader “digital fatigue” trend, where readers seek tangible experiences that digital platforms can’t replicate. By reviving a historic voice, the publication reconnects longtime fans and introduces new audiences to curated, long‑form sci‑fi analysis, potentially reshaping how niche media monetize through limited‑edition print. The move also signals to publishers that heritage brands can find viable business models by blending nostalgia with contemporary cultural relevance.

## How to think about it
Treat the annual issue as a cultural barometer rather than a regular news source. If you value physical media, consider pre‑ordering early to secure a copy and support the model. Use the magazine’s long‑form essays to gauge emerging themes in sci‑fi storytelling, which often foreshadow real‑world tech discussions. Finally, balance enthusiasm with critical eye: enjoy the nostalgia, but assess whether the content offers fresh insight beyond its legacy.

<Callout type="warning">Because the print run is limited, resale prices may surge on secondary markets; avoid speculative buying if you only want the content.</Callout>
