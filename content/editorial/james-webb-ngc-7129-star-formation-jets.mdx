On November 16, 2026 NASA released the latest Astronomy Picture of the Day, featuring an infrared portrait of the star‑forming region NGC 7129 captured by the James Webb Space Telescope. The image reveals a bustling nursery where dozens of young stars illuminate clouds of gas and dust in striking gold and red hues. Most striking are the narrow, high‑speed jets and winds that carve filamentary structures through the surrounding material. By exposing these dynamic processes, the picture offers a fresh window into how stars grow and interact with their environment.
<Callout type="takeaway">JWST shows that stellar jets play a decisive role in shaping the architecture of emerging star clusters.</Callout>
## What happened
The James Webb Space Telescope observed NGC 7129 in the near‑ and mid‑infrared, penetrating the opaque dust that hides the region in visible light.

The resulting image displays a gradient of stellar ages: the gold‑colored gas on the left hosts relatively mature protostars, while the red‑tinged area on the right contains younger objects still accreting material.

Numerous collimated jets emerge from the youngest stars, pushing against the surrounding gas and creating the observed filamentary red structures.
## Why it matters
Understanding how jets and winds regulate the flow of material in star‑forming clouds is essential for models that predict stellar masses and cluster evolution. The image provides direct evidence that feedback from newborn stars can reshape their environment, influencing the efficiency of star formation and the eventual distribution of stars in galaxies. Astronomers, modelers, and educators gain a concrete example to test theories of stellar feedback and to illustrate the dynamic nature of cosmic nurseries.

## How to think about it
When examining star‑forming regions, start by mapping infrared emission to locate embedded protostars, then overlay jet tracers (e.g., emission lines) to assess feedback. Compare multiple wavelengths to separate heating effects from mechanical shaping. Use JWST snapshots as benchmarks for simulations, but always test models against a diverse set of clouds.
<Callout type="warning">Do not assume every filament in star‑forming clouds is created by jets; other forces such as turbulence and magnetic fields also shape the gas.</Callout>
