On 10 November 2026 OpenAI’s language model released a proof of the Quantum Circuit Lower Bound conjecture, a problem that has resisted classical techniques for decades. The proof was posted alongside the company’s bulk release of over 700 mathematical manuscripts. Peer reviewers have begun to verify the argument, noting that the AI leveraged quantum‑aware complexity theory in novel ways. If validated, the result reshapes expectations for what automated systems can achieve in theoretical computer science.

## What happened
OpenAI uploaded a repository containing the formal proof, complete with definitions, lemmas, and a final theorem statement. The proof asserts that any quantum circuit of depth d requires at least Ω(d) gates to compute certain Boolean functions, confirming the longstanding lower‑bound conjecture. Reviewers highlighted that the AI employed a hybrid approach: it first generated candidate lemmas using a transformer‑based reasoning engine, then refined them through a symbolic verifier that respects quantum circuit semantics.

The release follows the earlier “mathocalypse” event, where the same system announced dozens of breakthroughs across pure mathematics. Unlike many of those claims, the quantum complexity proof has attracted immediate scrutiny from both computer scientists and physicists, accelerating the peer‑review cycle.

## Why it matters
A validated lower bound limits how efficiently quantum algorithms can outperform classical ones, informing hardware designers about realistic performance ceilings. It also proves that AI can navigate the intricate landscape of quantum computational theory, suggesting future collaborations where machines explore conjectures that blend physics and computer science. The broader research community must now decide how to integrate AI‑generated arguments into the standard validation pipeline.
