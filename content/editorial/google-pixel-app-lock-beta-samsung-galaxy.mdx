Mobile privacy just got a surprise upgrade. Google announced today that its Pixel phones will officially re‑release the long‑awaited App Lock feature in the Android 17 QPR3 Beta 3 build. At the same time, Samsung’s One UI 9.5 beta for the Galaxy S27 series already ships with the same capability, making Samsung users the first to experience it. The staggered rollout highlights how OEMs are racing to embed granular app‑level security into the Android ecosystem. For users who juggle work and personal data, the timing couldn’t be more relevant.

<Callout type="takeaway">App Lock’s return signals a renewed focus on on‑device privacy, with Samsung beating Pixel to the punch.</Callout>

## What happened
App Lock first appeared in an August build of Android 17 QPR2’s beta, letting users hide apps behind a biometric or PIN and suppress notifications, widgets, and AI‑generated data. Google pulled the feature from later QPR2 updates to polish the implementation, as noted by a Google Community Engagement Manager on Reddit, before re‑introducing it in QPR3 Beta 3 last month and officially releasing it today for Pixel devices.

Samsung’s One UI 9.5 beta, which is built on Android 17 QPR2, also includes App Lock support. This means the Galaxy S27 series will ship with the feature out of the box, while Pixel phones won’t receive the stable version until early 2027, likely around March. Older Galaxy devices are slated to get One UI 9.5 in May, following the typical rollout pattern.

## Why it matters
The re‑introduction of App Lock reflects growing user demand for granular, on‑device privacy controls amid increasing data‑driven services. By offering biometric or PIN protection at the app level, both Google and Samsung give users a way to shield sensitive information from accidental exposure and from AI services that scrape app content. The staggered availability also underscores the competitive dynamics among Android OEMs, where early feature adoption can become a differentiator for flagship devices.

## How to think about it
If you rely on apps that contain personal or work‑related data, enable App Lock as soon as the beta update arrives. Add high‑risk apps—banking, messaging, health—to the lock list via a long‑press on the icon. Pair the lock with a strong biometric (face or fingerprint) or a PIN that isn’t used elsewhere. Keep an eye on the stable Android 17 release schedule; once the feature graduates from beta, you can trust it for long‑term security. For devices that won’t receive the feature until 2027, consider third‑party app‑locking solutions as a stopgap.

<Callout type="warning">Beta builds can introduce bugs; don’t rely on App Lock for critical security until the stable release.</Callout>
