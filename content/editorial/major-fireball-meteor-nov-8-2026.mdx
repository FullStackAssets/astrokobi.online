The night of November 8, 2026 offered a spectacular sight to skywatchers across Europe. A bright fireball raced east‑to‑west over the northern horizon, briefly outshining the twilight glow above the Dolomite Alps. NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day posted a high‑resolution image of the meteor streaking beneath Ursa Major. Reports flooded the American Meteor Society and the International Meteor Organization, marking it as one of the most widely observed fireballs in recent years. Its connection to the Northern Taurid shower links the flash to dust left by comet Encke, reminding us of the dynamic environment surrounding Earth.

## What happened
The APOD image shows a snowy mountain pass in Italy’s Dolomite Alps taken in sub‑freezing conditions, with the village of La Villa illuminated below. During the long exposure, a luminous fireball crossed the frame from east to west, appearing just above the familiar shape of the Big Dipper. The event was witnessed across multiple European countries, and trajectory analysis suggests an origin near the radiant of the Northern Taurid meteor shower, which is associated with debris from comet Encke.

## Why it matters
Fireballs of this brightness provide rare, high‑energy samples of meteoroid material that can survive atmospheric entry, offering clues about the composition of ancient cometary dust. The surge of public reports enriches global meteor databases, improving orbit calculations and helping predict future activity. Moreover, the visual impact engages a broad audience, reinforcing the relevance of planetary defense initiatives and the need for continuous sky monitoring.
