## What happened

The release followed a controversial claim earlier that month that OpenAI had solved the Navier–Stokes existence and smoothness problem under a specific external‑force condition. Mathematicians quickly pointed out that the Navier–Stokes paper relied heavily on prior human work, prompting heightened scrutiny of the new quantum‑complexity manuscript.

Researchers worldwide have begun a systematic review, posting preliminary comments on pre‑print servers and organizing workshops to dissect the proof’s structure, its reliance on quantum information theory, and the underlying algorithmic assumptions.

## Why it matters
A verified proof would reshape our understanding of what quantum computers can efficiently compute, influencing cryptography, optimization, and the theoretical limits of simulation. It also signals that large‑scale language models can generate arguments at the frontier of mathematics, potentially accelerating discovery cycles. However, the episode underscores a gap: AI can produce plausible proofs faster than peer review can validate them, raising questions about trust, attribution, and the future role of human expertise.
