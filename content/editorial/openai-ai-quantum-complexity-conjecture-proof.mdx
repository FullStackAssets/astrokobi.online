The result follows the massive October 2026 bulk release of 722 mathematical manuscripts that sparked the so‑called “mathocalypse.” Demonstrating that an AI can settle a deep question in quantum complexity suggests a shift in how theoretical breakthroughs may be generated.

## What happened
On 11 November 2026 OpenAI uploaded a pre‑print titled “Proof of the Quantum Circuit Lower Bound Conjecture” to its mathematics repository. The paper outlines a constructive argument that any quantum circuit achieving a certain computational advantage must have depth at least Ω(log n), matching the conjectured bound.

The proof builds on techniques introduced in the October bulk release, extending them with a new “entanglement‑compression” lemma. Independent researchers have begun a verification process, posting initial comments on the repository’s issue tracker and planning formal peer review.

## Why it matters
Resolving the conjecture clarifies the limits of quantum advantage, informing both algorithm designers and policymakers who assess the timeline for practical quantum computers. It also demonstrates that large‑scale language models can navigate abstract algebraic reasoning, potentially accelerating discovery across physics, chemistry, and cryptography.
