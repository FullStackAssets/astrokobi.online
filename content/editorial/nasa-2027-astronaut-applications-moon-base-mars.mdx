NASA has opened its astronaut application window for the class that will begin training in 2027. U.S. citizens have until 11:59 p.m. EST on Dec 3 to submit their materials. The new cohort will be tasked with lunar surface operations, building a Moon Base, and laying groundwork for crewed missions to Mars. With more than 8,000 applicants in the previous round, the selection will shape the next chapter of human spaceflight.

<Callout type=\"takeaway\">The 2027 astronaut class will be the first to train specifically for sustained lunar presence and future Mars voyages.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA announced that applications are now being accepted for its next astronaut class, targeting missions that return humans to the Moon, construct a permanent lunar outpost, and eventually send crews to Mars. The deadline is set for 11:59 p.m. EST on Dec 3, after which the agency will review submissions and aim to make final selections in late 2027.

The agency’s last recruitment cycle in 2024 yielded more than 8,000 applications, from which ten candidates were chosen in 2025 and are currently completing two years of training. Since the 1960s, NASA has selected 370 individuals as astronaut candidates, underscoring the prestige and competitiveness of the program.

## Why it matters
Training a new generation of astronauts directly supports NASA’s Artemis lunar‑base objectives and the long‑term goal of crewed Mars exploration, both of which require specialized skills in low‑gravity operations, habitat construction, and autonomous decision‑making. The cohort will also drive technological innovation, from life‑support systems to in‑situ resource utilization, while serving as a public inspiration that sustains political and financial backing for deep‑space endeavors.

## How to think about it
Prospective applicants should assess their technical expertise, physical fitness, and willingness to commit to years of rigorous training. Aligning career goals with NASA’s Artemis and Mars roadmaps can strengthen an application, as can gaining experience in robotics, EVA, or habitat design. Candidates who are uncertain should explore related pathways such as commercial spaceflight programs or advanced research positions that keep them engaged with the evolving exploration ecosystem.

<Callout type=\"warning\">Submitting an incomplete application or missing the Dec 3 deadline will result in automatic disqualification.</Callout>
