NASA announced a suite of awards at the MAX POWER aerospace expo, targeting the agency’s Moon Base ambitions at the lunar South Pole. Eight commercial firms were selected for the next phase of the CLPS 2 initiative, a 10‑year, multiple‑award contract worth up to $10 billion. The CLPS 2 framework expands the existing CLPS 1.0 contract from $2.6 billion to $4.2 billion to fund early Moon Base objectives. Among the funded projects is a nuclear‑powered rover called PROMISE, alongside new robotic delivery and surface infrastructure concepts. Together, these steps aim to have the first elements of a permanent outpost waiting for returning astronauts.

<Callout type="takeaway">NASA’s new $10 billion CLPS 2 awards fast‑track a permanent Moon Base at the South Pole.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA shared the announcements on Saturday, emphasizing a “mission‑by‑mission” approach to building a Moon Base. The agency selected eight companies to compete for future lunar surface delivery missions under the CLPS 2 contract, which allows indefinite‑quantity deliveries of science, technology, and infrastructure payloads. The CLPS 2 master contract spans ten years and carries a maximum ceiling of $10 billion, while the original CLPS 1.0 contract was increased from $2.6 billion to $4.2 billion to support Phase One objectives.

In addition to the delivery services, NASA funded the development of PROMISE, a nuclear‑powered rover designed for long‑duration surface operations, and new launch and orbital transfer services. These investments also cover surface infrastructure needed to support a sustained presence at the Moon’s South Pole, aligning with the agency’s broader Artemis roadmap.

## Why it matters
The awards shift NASA’s focus from short‑term exploration missions to building the logistics backbone required for a permanent outpost. By leveraging commercial partners for cargo landers, the agency reduces risk and cost while accelerating technology maturation. A nuclear rover like PROMISE could enable extended traverses in the harsh polar environment, opening new scientific opportunities and supporting habitat construction.

## How to think about it
Treat the CLPS 2 awards as the first layer of a supply chain that will support habitats, power systems, and scientific payloads. Track which companies win lander contracts, as their design choices will dictate payload constraints for future missions. Consider the regulatory and safety implications of deploying nuclear power on the lunar surface, and monitor how NASA integrates these systems with planned Artemis crew flights.
