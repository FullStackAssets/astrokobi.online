On the night of November 8, 2026, NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day highlighted a striking fireball blazing across the Italian Dolomites. The meteor was caught serendipitously in a long‑exposure shot taken from a mountain pass on November 14, 2017, flashing east to west beneath Ursa Major. Observers across Europe reported the event, and trajectory analysis ties it to the Northern Taurid meteor shower, a stream linked to comet Encke. Such a well‑documented fireball offers a rare glimpse into the composition and dynamics of a long‑period meteoroid stream.

## What happened
The APOD image shows a snowy Alpine pass in Italy’s Dolomite range at twilight, with the village of La Villa below and the Big Dipper of Ursa Major overhead. During the camera’s exposure the meteor streaked east‑to‑west across the northern horizon, its bright trail captured against the fading pink sky.

European skywatchers logged the fireball, and the International Meteor Organization confirmed a track consistent with a radiant near the Northern Taurid shower. The shower originates from dust left by Encke’s comet, and the fireball’s path over Germany matches the expected trajectory for Taurid meteoroids in early November.

## Why it matters
A fireball recorded in both visual and photographic form adds precise positional data that can refine the orbital elements of the Taurid stream. Better orbital models improve predictions of future encounters and help assess any potential impact risk from larger Taurid fragments. Publicly shared images also spark widespread interest in meteoritic science, encouraging citizen‑science reporting that enriches professional datasets.
