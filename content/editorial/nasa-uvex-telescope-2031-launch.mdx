A new window onto the cosmos is about to open when NASA’s UVEX (UltraViolet EXplorer) lifts off in 2031. The mission will conduct the first comprehensive all‑sky survey in both near and far ultraviolet wavelengths. Led by Caltech, UVEX is designed to map the UV sky with unprecedented depth and resolution. By filling the ultraviolet gap left by existing observatories, the telescope promises fresh insights into stellar activity, galaxy evolution, and the habitability of exoplanets.

<Callout type="takeaway">UVEX will deliver the first complete ultraviolet map of the sky, unlocking phenomena invisible to other telescopes.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA announced that the UVEX spacecraft will launch in 2031 as an ultraviolet‑focused observatory. The mission, managed by Caltech, will perform an all‑sky survey covering near‑ and far‑UV bands, providing higher sensitivity and angular resolution than previous UV missions.

UVEX’s survey strategy involves scanning the entire celestial sphere over a three‑year primary mission, generating a catalog of millions of UV sources. Its data will be integrated with observations from the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, the Vera Rubin Observatory, and ESA’s Euclid mission to enable multi‑wavelength studies.

The telescope’s payload includes a wide‑field UV imager and a spectrograph designed to capture transient events such as stellar flares and supernova shock breakouts, phenomena that are largely invisible in optical light.

## Why it matters
Ultraviolet photons trace energetic processes that shape star formation, planetary atmospheres, and the interstellar medium. By mapping these emissions across the whole sky, UVEX will reveal the frequency of stellar flares, the distribution of hot gas in galaxies, and the early stages of supernova explosions.

Scientists will be able to refine models of exoplanet habitability, since UV radiation drives atmospheric chemistry and erosion. The mission also bridges a decade‑long gap since the GALEX survey, ensuring continuity in UV astronomy for future generations.

## How to think about it
Treat UVEX as a foundational layer for any astrophysical analysis that involves high‑energy processes. When building models of stellar evolution or exoplanet atmospheres, incorporate the upcoming UV catalog to account for radiation environments. Follow the mission’s data release schedule, and combine UV measurements with optical and infrared surveys to achieve a holistic view of each target.

<Callout type="warning">Early UVEX data will undergo extensive calibration; avoid drawing definitive scientific conclusions until the vetted data releases are available.</Callout>
