In early November 2026, an OpenAI‑trained transformer announced a formal proof of a major conjecture in quantum computational complexity. The conjecture, which has guided research on the limits of quantum versus classical computation for decades, was finally settled by a machine‑generated proof posted to the company’s public repository. The proof was peer‑reviewed by a small group of complexity theorists who confirmed its logical validity, marking the first time an AI has closed a foundational open problem in quantum theory. This breakthrough signals a shift in how mathematics and theoretical physics may be explored, with AI moving from heuristic assistance to autonomous discovery.

<Callout type="takeaway">AI can now autonomously resolve deep quantum complexity questions, changing the frontier of theoretical research.</Callout>

## What happened
On November 8, 2026, OpenAI released a 120‑page LaTeX manuscript titled “Proof of the Quantum Complexity Separation Conjecture” on GitHub. The document contains a sequence of lemmas, each generated by the model’s internal theorem‑proving engine, and a final theorem that establishes a super‑polynomial gap between BQP and the Polynomial Hierarchy under standard oracle assumptions.

The conjecture, long regarded as a litmus test for the power of quantum computation, asserts that there exist problems efficiently solvable by quantum computers that cannot be solved by any classical algorithm within the Polynomial Hierarchy. The AI’s proof constructs an explicit oracle and demonstrates, via a reduction, that any classical algorithm in PH would require exponential resources.

After the release, a panel of ten complexity theorists from leading universities spent two weeks checking the logical steps. Their consensus report, posted alongside the manuscript, concluded that the argument is mathematically sound, though they note that the proof’s style is unconventional and relies heavily on automated lemma generation.

## Why it matters
The result reshapes expectations for both quantum computing and AI research. It confirms that quantum advantage is not merely a practical observation but can be grounded in rigorous complexity separations, bolstering investment in quantum hardware. At the same time, it demonstrates that AI systems can navigate abstract mathematical landscapes without human intuition, suggesting a new paradigm where machines generate and verify deep theory.

## How to think about it
Treat AI‑generated proofs as collaborative drafts. First, run automated proof checkers to catch syntactic errors. Second, allocate domain experts to audit the logical flow and translate key insights into human‑readable language. Finally, consider the result as a hypothesis to be tested experimentally, especially when it informs quantum algorithm design.
