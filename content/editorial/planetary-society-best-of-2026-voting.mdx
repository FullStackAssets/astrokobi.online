The Planetary Society has launched its annual Best of 2026 awards, opening member voting for the year’s most impactful space science missions and society achievements. The call invites members to nominate and vote in categories such as Best Performance by a Space Science Mission and Best Planetary Society Accomplishment. Voting runs through Nov. 30, with results slated for early December. This crowdsourced recognition highlights the community’s role in shaping the narrative of space exploration. It also provides a snapshot of which projects captured public imagination and scientific acclaim in 2026.

<Callout type="takeaway">Member voting now decides which 2026 space milestones will be celebrated as the year’s best.</Callout>

## What happened
The Planetary Society posted a voting page on its website on Nov 2, 2026, announcing the Best of 2026 awards and listing several categories for members to consider. The announcement emphasizes that voting remains open until Nov 30 and that the organization will email the results in early December.

In addition to the two headline categories, the Society encourages members to suggest additional favorites through its online community, fostering discussion among enthusiasts. The call also prompts non‑members to join the Society to participate, linking to the sign‑up page.

## Why it matters
The awards serve as a barometer of public and professional enthusiasm for specific missions, potentially influencing future funding and policy decisions. Recognizing achievements also reinforces the value of collaborative efforts between scientists, engineers, and the public. By spotlighting successful missions, the Society helps maintain momentum for upcoming projects and inspires the next generation of space explorers.

## How to think about it
When evaluating nominations, consider both the mission’s scientific return and its broader cultural resonance. Look for projects that advanced technology, delivered new data, or captured public imagination through media coverage. Weigh these factors against the Society’s goals of education and advocacy to decide which achievements truly merit recognition.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the award results represent an exhaustive ranking of all 2026 space activities.</Callout>
