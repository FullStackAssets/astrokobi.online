Los Angeles welcomed a new landmark today as the California Science Center opened its Samuel Oschin Air & Space Center. The centerpiece is the retired space shuttle Endeavour, displayed upright in launch position at nearly 200 feet tall. Visitors can walk beneath the shuttle, explore interactive exhibits, and even sit in a replica cockpit. The opening marks the culmination of more than three decades of planning and construction, and it promises to draw global audiences to Los Angeles.

<Callout type="takeaway">The new center makes the iconic shuttle Endeavour accessible to millions, turning a historic artifact into an immersive learning experience.</Callout>

## What happened
The California Science Center officially opened the Samuel Oschin Air & Space Center to the public on November 13, after over thirty years of design, fundraising, and construction. The 200‑foot‑tall wing houses Endeavour in a launch‑position display, allowing visitors to view the shuttle from the ground, underneath its engines, and from an elevated platform at the nose.

Interactive stations surround the shuttle, including a landing‑simulator, a replica cockpit you can sit in, and displays of shuttle tires and other space‑flight hardware. The exhibit also lets guests peer inside the orbiter to see the robotic arm and internal systems, offering a hands‑on perspective on shuttle operations.

## Why it matters
By placing Endeavour in a highly accessible, interactive setting, the center transforms a static museum piece into a dynamic educational hub. The exhibit is expected to boost tourism in Los Angeles, generate significant revenue for the museum, and serve as a “force multiplier” for STEM outreach, inspiring the next generation of engineers and scientists.

## How to think about it
Plan your visit early, especially if you want to experience the shuttle interior or the simulator, as timed tickets can fill quickly. Educators should incorporate the exhibit into lesson plans before the trip, using the center’s online resources to frame key concepts in orbital mechanics and engineering. Treat the experience as a catalyst: encourage visitors to follow up with local maker spaces or school clubs to translate inspiration into projects.

<Callout type="warning">Do not touch any displayed hardware or climb on the shuttle structure; doing so can damage irreplaceable artifacts and violate museum policy.</Callout>
