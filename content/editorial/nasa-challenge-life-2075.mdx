Humanity has always imagined how life will look centuries from now, and NASA is turning that imagination into a crowdsourced research effort. On November 13, 2026, the agency opened the “Life and the World in 2075” challenge, inviting anyone to submit conceptual ideas about daily life and aerospace systems in the year 2075. The call targets broad, non‑technical visions that could inform future aviation, space travel, and societal trends. By tapping a global audience, NASA hopes to surface perspectives that its engineers and planners might otherwise miss.

<Callout type="takeaway">NASA is using public imagination to shape long‑term aerospace visions for 2075.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA’s Transformational Tools and Technologies (TTT) program announced the “Public Input on Life and the World in 2075” challenge, with registration opening on November 13, 2026. The initiative is hosted on the CrowdPlat platform, where participants can register and submit their ideas.

The challenge asks for conceptual, not technical, ideas across four thematic topics that explore how everyday life and aerospace systems might evolve by 2075. Submissions are meant to spark creative thinking about future aviation, space habitats, and the broader societal context in which they operate.

## Why it matters
A forward‑looking vision of life in 2075 can guide NASA’s long‑range technology roadmaps, ensuring that research aligns with societal aspirations and emerging needs. Public participation broadens the pool of ideas beyond the agency’s internal experts, potentially uncovering novel concepts for propulsion, habitats, or human‑machine interaction. The insights gathered may influence policy, funding priorities, and collaborative projects that shape the next half‑century of space exploration.

## How to think about it
Treat the challenge as a brainstorming exercise rather than a design competition. Start by envisioning everyday scenarios in 2075—how people travel, work, and interact with technology. Then map those scenarios onto possible aerospace solutions, such as ultra‑fast suborbital transport, autonomous space habitats, or AI‑driven mission planning. Keep ideas high‑level, focusing on the problem being solved and the societal benefit, and avoid detailed engineering specifications.
