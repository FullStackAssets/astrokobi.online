When a team of researchers asked 208 volunteers to brainstorm alternate uses for everyday objects, they discovered a striking split between human and machine collaborators. Participants paired with ChatGPT produced twice as many suggestions as those working with another person, confirming AI’s speed at generating sheer volume. However, the ideas from human duos were judged far more original, revealing that rapid output does not equal creative quality. The findings challenge the hype that generative AI can replace collaborative creativity in workplaces and research labs.

<Callout type="takeaway">AI can flood you with ideas, but human teams still win on originality.</Callout>

## What happened
Cognitive scientists designed an alternate‑uses task, asking participants to list as many novel functions for a common item (e.g., a brick) as possible. The 208 participants were randomly assigned to work either with another person or with ChatGPT, and each pair completed the task in the same time frame.

The AI‑paired groups generated roughly double the number of responses and wrote longer entries, confirming that large language models excel at producing high‑volume output quickly. In contrast, the human‑paired groups produced fewer items, but independent raters scored their ideas as significantly more original on a standard creativity rubric.

The study also noted that AI‑generated text tended to be verbose, with only about 42 % of the words delivering substantive content, echoing earlier findings about generative‑AI padding.

## Why it matters
If organizations lean on AI for idea generation without human curation, they risk filling pipelines with quantity‑heavy but low‑novelty concepts, potentially stifling breakthrough innovation in product design, research, and strategy. Human collaboration remains essential for weaving unexpected connections and delivering the originality that drives competitive advantage.

## How to think about it
Treat AI as a catalyst, not a substitute: start sessions by prompting the model for a wide‑range list, then allocate dedicated time for human partners to critique, combine, and refine those ideas. Prioritize a two‑step workflow—quantity first, quality second—to capture AI’s speed while preserving human originality.

<Callout type="warning">Do not let AI replace human collaboration when originality is critical.</Callout>
