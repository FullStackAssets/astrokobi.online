The Empire State Building was bathed in a golden glow on November 17, 2026, as NASA marked a historic moment for Voyager 1. The iconic New York skyscraper was illuminated to celebrate the probe’s approach to one light‑day away from Earth, a distance no human‑made object had previously reached. Voyager 1, launched in 1977, carries a gold‑plated phonograph record that encodes sounds and images of our planet. The visual tribute links a 20th‑century cultural artifact to a 21st‑century deep‑space milestone.

<Callout type="takeaway">Voyager 1’s one‑light‑day milestone was honored with a gold‑lit Empire State Building, highlighting the mission’s cultural resonance.</Callout>

## What happened
On the evening of November 17, 2026, the Empire State Building’s exterior lighting system was programmed to emit a steady gold hue. NASA coordinated the illumination to coincide with Voyager 1’s trajectory as it passed the one‑light‑day mark, approximately 25.9 billion kilometres from Earth.

The gold color was chosen to echo the Golden Record—a 12‑inch gold‑plated copper disc attached to Voyager that contains curated sounds and images of Earth. The record, a time capsule intended for any extraterrestrial finders, has become an emblem of humanity’s attempt to communicate across interstellar space.

## Why it matters
Celebrating Voyager’s milestone with a city‑wide visual signal transforms an abstract astronomical distance into a tangible experience for the public, reinforcing the relevance of long‑duration space missions. It also spotlights the Golden Record, reminding audiences that space exploration carries cultural as well as scientific payloads. Such high‑visibility events can catalyze interest in STEM fields and support future funding for deep‑space research.
