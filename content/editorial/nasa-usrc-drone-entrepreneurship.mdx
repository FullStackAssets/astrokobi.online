NASA’s University Student Research Challenge (USRC) gave a team of Michigan State engineering students a grant to explore next‑generation drone technology. Five years later, graduate Gavin Gardner has turned that research into AG3 Labs, a company that builds drones for military training exercises. The program’s blend of technical and entrepreneurial training proved pivotal in launching his startup. This story illustrates how targeted university funding can seed future aerospace innovators.
<Callout type=\"takeaway\">Combining engineering grants with entrepreneurship training can accelerate the creation of aerospace startups.</Callout>
## What happened
In 2021, NASA announced the USRC, offering multi‑year grants to university teams that could demonstrate both aeronautical research and a viable commercial pathway. Gavin Gardner, then a sophomore at Michigan State University, led a project on autonomous drone navigation under this program.
The experience forced the team to manage budgets, seek crowdfunding, and pitch their work—skills that later helped them launch AG3 Labs in 2024. Today, AG3 Labs supplies high‑fidelity drones for simulated combat training, and NASA’s USRC manager Steven Holz cites the venture as a model of the challenge’s long‑term impact.
## Why it matters
The initiative bridges the gap between academic research and market‑ready technology, creating a pipeline of skilled innovators for the aerospace sector. By exposing students to real‑world business challenges, the USRC cultivates a workforce capable of advancing both civilian and defense aviation. Successful spin‑outs like AG3 Labs demonstrate how early‑stage funding can translate into capabilities that strengthen national security and drive industry growth.

## How to think about it
Universities should embed entrepreneurship modules into engineering curricula, mirroring the USRC’s dual focus. Students can seek mentorship from industry partners early, treating grant proposals as business plans. Policymakers might expand funding pools to lower the barrier for more teams, while ensuring robust support structures for post‑grant commercialization.
<Callout type=\"warning\">Do not assume a grant guarantees startup success; rigorous market validation and sustainable financing remain essential.</Callout>
