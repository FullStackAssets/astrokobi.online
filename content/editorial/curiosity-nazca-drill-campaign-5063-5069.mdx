During sols 5063‑5069, Curiosity intensified its study of the Nazca drill site, home to unusual disk‑shaped crystal formations. The rover delivered drill cores to the Sample Analysis for Mars (SAM) instrument, performed ChemCam LIBS scans of several crystal targets, and captured high‑resolution mosaics with MAHLI and Mastcam. These observations aim to decode the mineralogy of chip‑shaped features that could reveal past water activity and inform future sample‑return decisions.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Nazca campaign links distinctive crystal morphologies to Mars’ aqueous history and upcoming sample‑return priorities.</Callout>

## What happened
The team completed a focused drill campaign at the Nazca hole, extracting bedrock that contains disk‑shaped, “potato‑chip” crystal forms. Samples were transferred to SAM for ongoing chemical analysis while the rover continued imaging the surrounding terrain.

ChemCam performed LIBS observations of the “Ahi Veremos” crystal form on Monday, followed by depth‑profile scans of the “Colca” and “Bermejo” targets on Wednesday. Remote Micro‑Imager (RMI) mosaics captured rock clusters below the Cordillera ridge, and MAHLI gathered a nighttime mosaic of the Nazca block using white‑light and ultraviolet LEDs. A Phobos eclipse was also recorded, and the rover drove away from the drill site to resume its southward trek.

The combined dataset includes spectral signatures, high‑resolution mosaics, and contextual environmental measurements such as dust‑devil surveys and horizon movies, providing a multi‑instrument view of the Nazca region.

## Why it matters
Understanding the composition of these chip‑shaped crystals can reveal whether they formed in the presence of liquid water, a key indicator of past habitability. Spectral data from ChemCam, paired with SAM’s chemical analyses, help constrain mineral phases that are otherwise ambiguous in imagery alone. The findings also guide target selection for the Mars Sample Return campaign, prioritizing sites that may hold preserved biosignatures.

## How to think about it
When evaluating Martian rock formations, start with morphology, then layer spectroscopic data to test mineral hypotheses. Use drill samples to validate remote sensing, but recognize that surface spectra may not capture subsurface diversity. Prioritize sites where multiple instruments converge on a consistent interpretation, especially when planning sample‑return targets.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume chip‑shaped crystals formed solely in water; alternative processes such as thermal fracturing can produce similar morphologies.</Callout>
