The Astronomy Picture of the Day released on 9 November 2026 features the bright star Rigel positioned above the Witch Head Nebula (IC 2118). The nebula stretches roughly 50 light‑years across and appears in striking blue tones. Rigel’s intense blue starlight illuminates the dust, while the dust grains preferentially scatter short wavelengths. This visual combination offers a vivid illustration of how light interacts with interstellar material. The image invites both casual viewers and researchers to consider the physics behind the glow.

<Callout type="takeaway">Rigel’s blue light and dust scattering turn IC 2118 into a vivid blue nebula, illustrating fundamental astrophysical processes.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA’s APOD page posted the image with an explanation that the nebula’s shape resembles a witch’s head. The feature highlights the relationship between the massive Orion star Rigel and the surrounding cloud.

IC 2118 lies about 50 light‑years across and is being eroded by the radiation pressure from Rigel. The blue hue results from a combination of Rigel’s blue photons and Rayleigh‑like scattering by dust grains, the same mechanism that makes Earth’s sky appear blue.

## Why it matters
Understanding how starlight scatters off dust informs models of interstellar medium composition and star‑formation environments. The nebula serves as a nearby laboratory for testing theories of grain size distribution and radiation feedback. Educators can use the image to demonstrate fundamental optics concepts, while astronomers gain a clear example of star‑cloud interaction.

## How to think about it
When evaluating nebular images, first identify the dominant illuminating source, then consider dust grain properties that affect scattering. Compare the nebula’s color to familiar phenomena such as Earth’s blue sky to gauge scattering efficiency. Use the observed size and distance to estimate mass and potential star‑formation activity.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the nebula’s shape precisely matches a witch’s head; human pattern recognition can impose misleading interpretations.</Callout>
