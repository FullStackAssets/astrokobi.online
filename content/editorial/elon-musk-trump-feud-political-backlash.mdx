Elon Musk’s feud with former President Donald Trump resurfaced between June and September 2025, turning the billionaire’s social‑media platforms into battlefields. The dispute began after Musk left the second Trump administration and publicly criticized the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, prompting a series of retaliatory posts on X and Truth Social. The conflict escalated when Trump told a Michigan rally, "Maybe it's time he went back to where he came from," and later the two appeared together at a memorial service, suggesting a brief thaw. Beyond the headlines, the feud cost the Trump campaign more than $50 million in legal fees and forced observers to reconsider how tech moguls wield political influence. Understanding this episode matters because it reveals the fragile alliance between Silicon Valley power and electoral politics.

## What happened
From June to September 2025, Musk and Trump exchanged attacks across X and Truth Social, a period marked by rapid, public escalation. Musk’s criticism of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act—legislation championed by Trump—triggered a series of retaliatory posts, culminating in Trump’s rally remark that hinted at personal animus. By September, the two appeared together at Charlie Kirk’s memorial service, signaling a temporary reconciliation but leaving lingering tension.

## Why it matters
The feud illustrates how a single tech CEO can become a flashpoint in national politics, amplifying partisan narratives and driving legal expenses that strain campaign finances. Stakeholders include voters who receive politicized content on platforms they use daily, investors watching Musk’s brand volatility, and policymakers grappling with regulation of powerful digital ecosystems.
