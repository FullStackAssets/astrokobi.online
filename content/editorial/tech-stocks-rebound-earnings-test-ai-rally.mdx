Tech stocks surged on Monday as the S&P 500 and Nasdaq found brief support after a sharp pullback. The rally was led by AI‑focused companies and chip makers, with the iShares Semiconductor ETF snapping a nine‑day slump. Investors see the bounce as a potential turning point, but the durability of the move hinges on this week’s heavyweight earnings. Analysts warn that fragile price action means the market could reverse quickly if results disappoint. The outcome will shape expectations for the broader technology sector and the AI trade.

<Callout type="takeaway">The coming earnings season will determine whether Monday’s bounce signals a sustainable recovery or a fleeting flare‑up.</Callout>

## What happened
The S&P 500 fell nearly 2% last week, its worst weekly decline since late June, while the Nasdaq dropped 4.5% over the same period. Monday’s rebound lifted both indices as AI‑related stocks rallied, and the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) broke a nine‑day slide. South Korean chip giants, represented by the iShares MSCI South Korea ETF (EWY), surged 4% after a week of steep losses.

Earnings pressure is mounting. So far, 47 S&P 500 companies have reported Q3 results, with 85% beating expectations and a blended earnings growth rate of 29%. Yet the bulk of tech earnings—including Meta Platforms, Tesla, and SpaceX—remain pending, and analysts stress that proof of AI‑driven growth will be essential.

## Why it matters
The tech sector’s performance is a bellwether for market sentiment, especially as AI investments drive valuation premiums. A strong earnings season could cement the bounce, encouraging risk‑on positioning and supporting the S&P 500 and Nasdaq. Conversely, weak results may validate concerns about an earnings‑expectation bubble, prompting a broader sell‑off and tightening credit conditions for growth firms.

## How to think about it
Treat the Monday rally as a data point, not a trend line. Prioritize companies that can demonstrate concrete AI revenue growth in their earnings calls. Maintain a diversified exposure to both AI leaders and broader tech to mitigate the risk of a dispersion of winners and losers. If earnings exceed expectations, consider modestly increasing positions; if they fall short, be prepared to tighten risk controls and protect capital.

<Callout type="warning">Do not dramatically increase exposure to tech stocks based solely on the Monday bounce; wait for earnings clarity.</Callout>
