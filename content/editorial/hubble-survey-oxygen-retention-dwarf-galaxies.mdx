Oxygen is a key tracer of how elements build up in galaxies. A recent Hubble‑based survey examined 37 nearby dwarf galaxies and found that only about 15 % of the oxygen forged by their stars remains inside the galaxies today. The rest appears to have been expelled into the surrounding circumgalactic medium. This result challenges the assumption that low‑mass systems retain most of their nucleosynthetic products. Understanding where the missing oxygen resides reshapes models of galactic feedback and chemical enrichment.

<Callout type="takeaway">Dwarf galaxies keep just a fraction of their produced oxygen, with most expelled into their halos.</Callout>

## What happened
The team combined Hubble imaging with archival Spitzer infrared data and radio observations from the Very Large Array and the Australian Telescope Compact Array. By measuring emission lines from ionized gas they derived the total oxygen mass produced by recent star formation and compared it to the oxygen present in gas, stars and dust.

Across the 37 galaxies the retained oxygen fraction averages roughly 15 %, with the bulk of the deficit inferred to reside in the circumgalactic medium. Core‑collapse supernovae dominate the oxygen production, while a smaller contribution comes from Type Ia and other low‑mass stellar endpoints.

## Why it matters
If dwarf galaxies routinely lose most of their freshly minted oxygen, models of chemical enrichment must account for strong outflows that transport metals into the circumgalactic and intergalactic media. This influences predictions of metal cooling, star‑formation efficiency, and the metallicity of future generations of stars in low‑mass systems. It also affects how we interpret the metal content of the broader universe, since dwarf galaxies are numerous contributors to the cosmic metal budget.

## How to think about it
Treat the 15 % retention figure as a baseline for low‑mass systems and ask how outflow strength, star‑formation burstiness, and halo mass modify that number. When building chemical‑evolution models, include a term for metal ejection that scales with supernova energy and galaxy escape velocity. Observationally, prioritize CGM absorption studies to verify where the missing oxygen resides.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the same oxygen loss fraction applies to massive spirals or elliptical galaxies without supporting data.</Callout>
