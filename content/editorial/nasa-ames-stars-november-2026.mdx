Each month, NASA’s Ames Research Center spotlights the individuals whose work fuels the agency’s boldest missions. In November 2026 the center honored Alyssa Villanueva, Steve Howell, and Samuel LeBlanc for their contributions to space biology, astronomical instrumentation, and airborne science flight planning. Their achievements illustrate how engineering excellence, scientific leadership, and innovative planning keep NASA at the forefront of exploration. Recognizing these leaders not only celebrates past successes but also signals the capabilities that will shape future missions.

<Callout type="takeaway">Celebrating these engineers underscores how interdisciplinary talent drives NASA’s exploration agenda.</Callout>

## What happened
Alyssa Villanueva, an engineer on the LEIA (Lunar Explorer Instrument for space biology Applications) team, was named the Space Biosciences Star for her technical excellence and relentless support of engineering validation testing. Steve Howell received recognition for his lifelong leadership of scientific teams and pioneering work in astronomical instrumentation, recently honored with the Catherine Wolfe Bruce Gold Medal. Samuel LeBlanc, a research scientist in the Earth Science Division, was celebrated for his innovative flight‑planning leadership that has positioned Ames at the forefront of airborne science missions.

## Why it matters
These recognitions highlight three critical pillars of NASA’s mission: the ability to conduct biology experiments in space, the development of cutting‑edge telescopic instruments, and the efficient planning of airborne science campaigns. Advances in space biology enable life‑support research for long‑duration missions, while improved instrumentation expands our view of the universe. Effective flight planning maximizes scientific return and reduces costs, directly influencing the feasibility of future exploratory flights.
