A strong cold front is set to sweep across southeast Texas this Sunday, bringing a mix of rain, wind, and isolated severe weather. Saturday afternoon will see clouds thickening, with showers beginning after sunset and intensifying into scattered rain on Sunday morning. By Sunday afternoon, heavy rain moves into the Houston area, and a wind advisory will be in effect from 9 AM to 3 PM, with gusts topping 30 mph. The combination of rain and wind raises the risk of flash flooding in low‑lying neighborhoods. Residents are urged to prepare backup plans and stay tuned to local alerts.

## What happened
Saturday afternoon clouds increase across southeast Texas, and isolated showers appear around 6 PM, becoming spotty to scattered by 8 PM. The rain chances grow after sunset, setting the stage for Sunday.

On Sunday morning, scattered rain spreads over Houston, with the strongest thunderstorms expected north of the city. By the afternoon, widespread heavy rain moves in, and the National Weather Service has issued a Wind Advisory for southern counties from 9 AM to 3 PM, with gusts that could exceed 30 mph.

While the overall severe weather threat remains low, hail, damaging winds, and an isolated tornado are possible. Flooding concerns focus on low‑lying areas and places with poor drainage, especially in The Woodlands southward. The storms are expected to move offshore by about 6 PM, leaving lingering showers into Monday, when a colder and windier day follows.
## Why it matters
The heavy rain threatens flash flooding in neighborhoods with poor drainage, potentially disrupting traffic, damaging property, and straining emergency services. Gusty winds above 30 mph can bring down tree limbs and power lines, leading to outages and safety hazards. Together, the rain‑wind combo challenges city planners and residents, highlighting the need for robust preparedness as climate patterns shift toward more volatile spring storms.

## How to think about it
Monitor the National Weather Service alerts throughout the day and keep a portable emergency kit with water, food, and a flashlight. If you live in a low‑lying area, consider sandbags or temporary barriers to divert water. Secure outdoor objects that could become projectiles in high winds, and have a backup power source for essential medical devices. Plan alternate routes in case main roads become flooded, and stay informed via local radio or trusted apps.
<Callout type="warning">Do not attempt to drive through flooded streets; wait for official clearance before traveling in affected zones.</Callout>
