As early November settles in, Houston is defying the typical seasonal chill. The latest forecast shows mostly sunny skies with highs in the low‑ to mid‑80s and only a 10‑20% chance of rain today. These warm conditions will linger through the weekend, but a stronger cold front is expected early next week, bringing a shift that could affect outdoor activities and energy demand.

<Callout type="takeaway">Houston will stay warm this week before a cooling front arrives early next week.</Callout>

## What happened

Radar this morning shows rain showers over the Gulf near Port Lavaca and Indianola, moving northeast toward Matagorda. For most of Harris County the rain probability is only 10‑20%, with partly cloudy skies expected and highs near 80 °F. Overnight lows should remain in the upper 60s, keeping the night relatively mild.

Friday is projected to be sunny with a high around 80 °F and a 20% chance of afternoon showers. Saturday’s outlook includes a few more clouds, highs in the low‑ to mid‑80s, and a 40% chance of rain that is likely to taper off by late afternoon, preserving trick‑or‑treating plans.

The warm, mid‑80s pattern is set to continue into early next week. Meteorologists anticipate a fair‑strong cold front to push through Tuesday or Wednesday, which should bring cooler air and a noticeable drop in temperature.

## Why it matters

Extended warmth reduces heating demand but raises humidity‑related discomfort and can strain the power grid during peak afternoon use. The weekend’s modest rain odds mean outdoor events, including Halloween activities, can proceed with minimal disruption. However, the approaching front could trigger rapid temperature swings, affecting vulnerable populations and prompting a quick shift in energy consumption patterns.

## How to think about it

Keep an eye on daily updates, especially Tuesday’s outlook, and schedule any outdoor work or celebrations for early in the week while temperatures are highest. Adjust HVAC settings to balance comfort with energy use, and have a light jacket ready for the cooler air expected after the front passes.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the cold front will bring heavy rain; current models still show only modest precipitation chances.</Callout>
