The Houston Rockets opened the season 5-1, and a quiet but noticeable upgrade has come from forward Jabari Smith Jr. While he isn’t shouldering superstar minutes, his shooting and scoring have taken clear steps forward. Smith is now hitting 43% of his three‑point attempts on 5.8 tries per game, a career high, and he’s creating his own looks more often. This incremental growth eases the load on stars like Kevin Durant and Alperen Şengün and could prove decisive in tighter contests. The change matters because opponents will focus on the Rockets’ marquee names, leaving Smith as a less‑targeted offensive option.

<Callout type="takeaway">Smith’s steady improvements give Houston a reliable secondary scorer without demanding a superstar role.</Callout>

## What happened
Smith has become the Rockets’ most dependable shooter, posting a 43% three‑point rate while attempting 5.8 threes per game. The uptick reflects both better ball movement from the team and his own work on shot selection and mechanics, allowing him to take tougher looks with confidence.

Defensively, Smith continues to contribute with 1.2 steals and 0.8 blocks per game, maintaining his reputation as a solid defender. His rebounding has slipped to five per contest, down from the eight he averaged in earlier seasons, suggesting a specific area for improvement.

## Why it matters
The Rockets rely heavily on Kevin Durant and Alperen Şengün for scoring; a secondary threat like Smith forces defenses to spread, creating more space for the stars. His ability to generate his own offense also mitigates the risk when Şengün misses time, preserving the team’s scoring balance. Over the long run, consistent incremental growth can evolve a role player into a dependable starter, shaping roster construction and salary decisions.

## How to think about it
Treat Smith as a developmental asset: monitor his shooting trends, encourage him to take more contested mid‑range attempts, and pair him with ball‑dominant teammates to maximize spacing. Coaches should design set plays that let him exploit mismatches while also assigning him defensive responsibilities that play to his strengths. Off the court, continued skill work on rebounding fundamentals can round out his game and increase his value.
