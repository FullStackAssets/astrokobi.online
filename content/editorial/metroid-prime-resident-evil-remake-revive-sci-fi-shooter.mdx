When Metroid Prime 4: Beyond launched on the Nintendo Switch in 2025, expectations were sky‑high after a five‑year development cycle. Instead, players encountered a game that felt like a relic, with underused side characters, an underwhelming bike mechanic, and an ending that left many unsatisfied. The lukewarm reception has sparked a debate about the franchise’s direction, especially as rival Nintendo titles like the Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake demonstrate the power of modernizing classics. Critics argue that a fresh take on the original Metroid Prime trilogy could restore the series’ signature sci‑fi shooter excitement. The conversation now centers on whether a Resident Evil‑style remake approach could deliver that revival.

<Callout type="takeaway">A modern remake of the Metroid Prime trilogy could revive the series’ sci‑fi shooter identity.</Callout>

## What happened
Metroid Prime 4: Beyond arrived in 2025 after five years of work at Retro Studios, the original creators of the Prime series. While the game preserved the franchise’s visual aesthetic, reviewers noted that its side characters felt superfluous, the new bike traversal added little strategic depth, and the narrative conclusion was broadly described as unsatisfying.

The series’ earlier entries, Metroid Prime 2 and 3, have never been officially remastered for the Switch, leaving a generation of players without easy access to the core trilogy. Meanwhile, Nintendo’s recent Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake on Switch 2 has shown how a classic can be revitalized with updated graphics, refined controls, and modernized pacing, sparking calls for a similar treatment of Metroid Prime.

Capcom’s Resident Evil remakes—starting with the 2002 overhaul and continuing with Resident Evil 2 and 4—demonstrate a successful formula: retain the original’s atmosphere while injecting contemporary gameplay standards. Proponents suggest applying that blueprint to the Metroid Prime trilogy, using the Prime 4 engine as a technical foundation.

## Why it matters
A well‑executed remake could re‑engage lapsed fans, attract new players, and reinforce Nintendo’s reputation for breathing new life into legacy titles. It would also address the franchise’s current identity crisis, where the series risks being perceived as stagnant or out‑of‑step with modern shooter expectations. Conversely, failing to modernize may widen the gap between Metroid and competing sci‑fi franchises that continue to innovate.

## How to think about it
Treat the remake as a platform for both preservation and innovation. Start by mapping the core gameplay loops that defined the original trilogy—exploration, atmospheric tension, and strategic combat—and ensure they survive unchanged. Then layer modern enhancements: tighter controls, updated enemy AI, and optional difficulty settings that respect both newcomers and veterans. Finally, position the project as a bridge, using the refreshed trilogy to gauge interest before committing to entirely new Metroid entries.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume a remake will automatically succeed; quality hinges on faithful design choices and meeting contemporary player standards.</Callout>
