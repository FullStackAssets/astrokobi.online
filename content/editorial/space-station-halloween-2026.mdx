When October rolls around, the International Space Station transforms into a floating costume party, proving that even in microgravity the human love of Halloween endures. Astronauts aboard the orbiting laboratory have been dressing up in improvised outfits—often fashioned from spare fabric, food wrappers, and other supplies found on the station. These celebrations are documented on NASA’s website each year, showing crew members posing in spooky, funny, and sometimes scientifically themed attire. Beyond the fun, the tradition offers a morale boost and a relatable story that connects the public to life aboard the ISS.

<Callout type="takeaway">Costume celebrations on the ISS highlight crew ingenuity and the importance of cultural rituals for long‑duration spaceflight.</Callout>

## What happened
Astronauts have been creating Halloween costumes using materials that are already aboard the station, such as fabric scraps from experiment bags, zip‑tie strings, and even printed circuit board panels. The outfits range from classic monsters to space‑themed characters, and the crew often coordinates group photos that are posted on NASA’s public channels.

NASA’s Office of Communications regularly curates these images, noting that the holiday has become an informal tradition since the station’s continuous occupancy began over 25 years ago. The celebrations are shared alongside scientific updates, reinforcing the station’s role as both a research platform and a human habitat.

## Why it matters
The festivities serve more than just entertainment; they provide a psychological lift for crews living in confined, isolated environments for months at a time. Shared cultural moments help maintain a sense of normalcy, strengthen team cohesion, and offer a vivid narrative that engages the public, fostering continued support for long‑term space endeavors.

## How to think about it
Mission planners should incorporate low‑impact cultural activities like holiday celebrations into crew schedules, allocating safe, designated spaces for costume assembly and photo sessions. A simple checklist—ensuring no loose items near ventilation, confirming that costumes do not obstruct controls, and scheduling the activity during low‑priority operational windows—can preserve safety while reaping morale benefits.

<Callout type="warning">Never attach costume elements to external hardware or block vents; safety always precedes celebration.</Callout>
