Samsung’s upcoming Galaxy S27 lineup has had its camera sensor roadmap leaked. The lower‑end S27 and S27+ will stick with the familiar 50 MP main sensor, while the new S27 Pro and the flagship Ultra adopt a 200 MP main sensor. Ancillary lenses remain unchanged, using the same 12 MP ultrawide and 3× telephoto modules seen on recent flagships. The reveal suggests modest evolutionary tweaks rather than a generational leap, a point that could shape buyer expectations.

<Callout type="takeaway">The S27 series upgrades the main camera only on the Pro and Ultra models, leaving the rest of the hardware largely unchanged.</Callout>

## What happened
The lower‑end S27 and S27+ will reuse the 50 MP main camera that Samsung has been testing with either a Sony IMX955 or a Samsung S5KGNG sensor—the same hardware found in the S26 series. Their ultrawide lens continues to use a 12 MP Sony IMX564 sensor, and the 3× telephoto relies on a Samsung S5K3K1 sensor. Front‑facing cameras will be either a Samsung S5K3LU or a Sony IMX874, mirroring previous generations.

For the newly introduced S27 Pro and the flagship S27 Ultra, Samsung plans a 200 MP main sensor built around the Samsung S5KHP6 chip. These two models will share this high‑resolution sensor while retaining the same supporting lenses as the lower models, meaning the only substantive hardware jump is the main sensor resolution.

## Why it matters
By keeping most of the camera stack identical across the lineup, Samsung reduces development risk and can price the base models competitively. However, the modest upgrade may disappoint consumers expecting a breakthrough in low‑light performance or new computational features. The 200 MP sensor on the Pro and Ultra could differentiate the premium tier, but its real‑world benefit depends on software processing and pixel‑binning efficiency.

## How to think about it
Treat the S27 series as an incremental refresh rather than a generational leap. If you prioritize resolution for large prints or cropping, the Pro and Ultra’s 200 MP sensor may be worth the premium. For everyday photography, the unchanged 50 MP main sensor and legacy lenses will perform similarly to the S26, so weigh the price difference against the marginal gains. Keep an eye on Samsung’s software updates, which often unlock more of a sensor’s potential after launch.
