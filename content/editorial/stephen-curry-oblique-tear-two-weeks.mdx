The Golden State Warriors announced that star guard Stephen Curry will miss at least the next two weeks after being diagnosed with an oblique tear. The injury was confirmed during practice on Monday, and medical staff expect a recovery period that sidelines him through several upcoming games. With the Warriors perched near the Western Conference playoff line, Curry’s absence forces a rapid tactical reshuffle. The development also spotlights how teams rely on star health to navigate the season’s final stretch.

## What happened

On November 13, Warriors practice staff identified a strain in Curry’s right oblique muscle. An MRI confirmed a tear, and the team’s medical report projected a minimum two‑week layoff.

Coach Steve Kerr confirmed that Curry will not travel for the upcoming games against the Lakers and the Suns, and the roster will rely on Klay Thompson, Andrew Wiggins, and the bench to fill scoring gaps.

The injury comes as Golden State holds a 45‑30 record, sitting fourth in the West, with the next ten games determining seeding.

## Why it matters

Curry’s scoring, playmaking, and floor spacing are central to the Warriors’ offensive identity; his absence could reduce the team’s win probability during a stretch of games against top Western opponents.
