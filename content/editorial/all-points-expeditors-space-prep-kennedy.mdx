The logistics chain that fuels every launch is getting a dedicated hub at the nation’s premier launch complex. All Points Logistics has announced a partnership with Expeditors International to serve as the exclusive logistics provider for its planned Space Prep Spaceport Logistics Center at Kennedy Space Center. The new 266,000‑square‑foot facility, slated to open by the end of 2027, will bring aerospace warehousing, inventory control, and launch‑campaign support under one roof. By co‑locating logistics expertise with launch pads, the partnership aims to reduce delays and improve mission readiness for both government and commercial space operators.

<Callout type="takeaway">On‑site, end‑to‑end logistics will turn supply‑chain bottlenecks into a mission‑readiness advantage for space launches.</Callout>

## What happened
All Points Logistics announced a partnership with Expeditors International of Washington, Inc. to act as the exclusive logistics provider for its Space Prep Spaceport Logistics Center at Kennedy Space Center, Florida. The collaboration positions Expeditors on‑site to deliver end‑to‑end mission logistics for government agencies and commercial space operators.

Expeditors will establish an on‑site presence offering aerospace warehousing, inventory control, transportation management, project cargo services, customs brokerage, supply‑chain visibility, and launch‑campaign support. The operation will serve operators at Kennedy Space Center, Cape Canaveral Space Force Station, and other launch locations.

As part of the Space Prep initiative, All Points plans to build a 266,000‑square‑foot Logistics Center on a 65‑acre site, located within five miles of the launch pads, with an adjacent 260,000‑square‑foot Spacecraft Processing Center. The facility is scheduled to open by the end of 2027.

## Why it matters
Bringing logistics services directly to the launch complex reduces the time and complexity of moving mission‑critical hardware, a common source of launch delays. By integrating customs brokerage and supply‑chain visibility, the hub can streamline regulatory compliance for both domestic and international payloads. The partnership also signals a maturation of the space supply chain, encouraging more commercial operators to plan missions from Kennedy with confidence that their hardware will be staged and managed efficiently.

## How to think about it
Space operators should map their critical hardware flow and identify which stages could benefit from the on‑site services Expeditors will provide. Early coordination with the Kennedy hub can secure warehousing slots and customs clearance well before a launch window. Smaller companies might negotiate shared‑use agreements to offset costs while still gaining access to the streamlined supply chain. Ultimately, integrating the hub into mission planning can turn logistics from a reactive task into a proactive element of launch strategy.
