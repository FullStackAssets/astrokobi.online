At a New York event on Nov. 12, AST SpaceMobile unveiled plans for Blacklift, a reusable launch vehicle designed to carry its own BlueBird satellites. The company claims the three‑stage rocket can lift 20 tons to low‑Earth orbit and will enable up to two launches per month. Executives say the vehicle could be operational within two years, a timeline that starkly contrasts with industry skepticism about a newcomer building a launch system from scratch. If true, Blacklift would give the firm a private launch pipeline amid a crowded and capacity‑constrained market. The announcement also signals a shift toward vertical integration for satellite operators.
<Callout type="takeaway">Blacklift could give AST SpaceMobile its own rapid, reusable launch cadence, but delivering a new rocket in two years is highly ambitious.</Callout>
## What happened
AST SpaceMobile’s chief executive Abel Avellan described Blacklift as a three‑stage, 20‑ton LEO vehicle that will use seven Firefly Miranda engines on the first stage, two Vira engines on the second, and a single Vira on the third. The design is intended to recover the first stage at low altitude, simplifying reuse compared with higher‑energy two‑stage rockets.
The company plans to launch twice a month, each flight carrying up to five BlueBird satellites. All structures are to be built from carbon‑composite materials, leveraging the firm’s experience in satellite manufacturing. No physical hardware was displayed; the presentation relied on engine videos from Firefly and computer‑generated animations.
## Why it matters
Securing an in‑house launch capability could shield AST SpaceMobile from the current bottleneck in commercial rideshare slots, accelerating its global broadband rollout. However, the promise of a new reusable rocket from a company whose core business is satellite communications challenges traditional aerospace development timelines and may reshape competitive dynamics if successful. Investors, regulators, and downstream users will watch closely to see whether the schedule and performance claims hold up.

## How to think about it
Treat Blacklift as a strategic bet: weigh the upside of guaranteed launch slots against the risk of delayed or underperforming hardware. Track milestones such as engine integration, first‑stage recovery tests, and composite tank certification. For investors, compare AST’s projected launch cost per kilogram with existing providers to gauge economic viability.
<Callout type="warning">Do not assume AST SpaceMobile will meet the two‑year launch target without independent verification of hardware progress.</Callout>
