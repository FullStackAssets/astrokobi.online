Using the James Webb Space Telescope, astronomers have catalogued 104 magnified stellar events in the Dragon galaxy, a distant system at redshift 0.725. The events were uncovered by comparing JWST images taken between 2022 and 2024, revealing rapid brightening as individual stars cross micro‑caustics created by the foreground cluster Abell 370. Because such caustic‑crossing flashes expose some of the most luminous stars and trace the distribution of dark matter, the finding reshapes our view of stellar populations in the early universe.

<Callout type="takeaway">JWST’s new catalog doubles the known caustic‑crossing events, sharpening probes of massive stars and dark matter in a distant galaxy.</Callout>

## What happened
The research team led by José María Palencia compared JWST near‑infrared images of the Dragon arc taken over a two‑year span, identifying 104 transient brightenings that match the signature of caustic‑crossing events. The Dragon galaxy lies about 6.5 billion light‑years away (redshift 0.725) and is stretched into an arc by the massive galaxy cluster Abell 370, whose gravity acts as a natural telescope.

When a background star drifts across a microcaustic—tiny distortions in the lensing field caused by individual stars and possibly dark matter in the cluster—its two lensed images merge, and the star’s apparent brightness can surge by hundreds to thousands of times for days to weeks before fading.

The findings were published in *Astronomy & Astrophysics* on September 30, and they more than double the previous record of 44 such detections in the same system.

## Why it matters
Caustic‑crossing events provide a rare window onto individual stars at cosmological distances, allowing astronomers to study the most massive and luminous stellar populations in the early universe. The frequency and distribution of these events also encode information about the small‑scale mass structure of the lensing cluster, offering indirect constraints on the nature of dark matter. By expanding the sample size, the new catalog improves statistical power for both stellar evolution models and dark‑matter substructure analyses, influencing how we interpret galaxy formation across cosmic time.

## How to think about it
Treat each brightening as a data point that links three ingredients: the background star’s intrinsic properties, the lensing geometry of Abell 370, and the intervening mass distribution (including dark matter). When evaluating the significance of any individual event, consider the event’s duration, peak magnification, and its location relative to known caustic structures. For broader scientific conclusions, aggregate the events to map the micro‑caustic network, then compare the inferred mass granularity with predictions from cold‑dark‑matter simulations.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume a brightening directly maps to a single isolated star without accounting for possible microlensing by cluster stars or sub‑halo dark‑matter structures.</Callout>
