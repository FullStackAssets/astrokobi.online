NASA is kicking off its inaugural MAX POWER aerospace expo and air show at Kennedy Space Center from Nov 6 to Nov 8. The three‑day event will feature the agency’s quiet‑supersonic X‑59 jet, Moon Base updates, and a public air show with flyovers. Visitors can meet astronauts, engineers, and NASA leadership during family‑friendly sessions. By gathering industry partners and the public, the expo aims to showcase technologies that will shape the next era of air and space travel. The timing aligns with NASA’s broader push to accelerate lunar exploration and next‑generation aviation.

<Callout type="takeaway">MAX POWER bundles cutting‑edge flight tech and lunar plans into a single public showcase.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA announced that MAX POWER will run from Friday, Nov. 6 through Sunday, Nov. 8, with gates opening at 9 a.m. EST on the weekend days. The schedule includes a nuclear media gaggle, opening air‑show remarks by Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson, and a live news conference at 5 p.m. on Nov. 7 to deliver the latest Moon Base update.

Key attractions include a display of the X‑59 quiet supersonic aircraft, demonstrations from external collaborators featuring Moon rovers and spacecraft, and an F‑5 flyover led by Administrator Jared Isaacman. The event also offers one‑on‑one media briefings, opportunities to interview NASA leaders, and family‑friendly activities throughout the Kennedy Space Center grounds.

Media credentialing for the expo is closed; approved journalists must request interviews in advance, and space at the news conference is limited on a first‑come, first‑served basis. Virtual participation is available for those unable to attend in person.

## Why it matters
MAX POWER serves as a public bridge between NASA’s cutting‑edge research and everyday audiences, helping to build support for ambitious programs like the lunar South‑Pole base. Showcasing the X‑59 highlights progress toward quieter supersonic travel, a technology that could reshape commercial aviation. By gathering industry partners, the expo accelerates collaboration on autonomous vehicles and space‑flight systems, potentially shortening development cycles.

The Moon Base briefing underscores NASA’s commitment to sustained lunar presence, a stepping stone for future Mars missions. Public exposure to these initiatives can influence policy funding and inspire the next generation of engineers and scientists.
