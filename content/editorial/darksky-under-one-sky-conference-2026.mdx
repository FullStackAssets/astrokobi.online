The night sky is fading for the majority of humanity, and a new virtual gathering aims to reverse that trend. On November 6‑7, 2026, DarkSky International will host the "Under One Sky" conference, a 24‑hour online event that brings together astronomers, ecologists, policymakers, and storytellers. Attendees will hear from experts on everything from urban lighting design to nocturnal insect health, all focused on preserving darkness. By making the conference fully virtual, the organization hopes to reach a global audience that can act locally. The stakes are high: without intervention, more of the planet will lose its view of the Milky Way.
<Callout type="takeaway">The Under One Sky conference provides a global platform to share actionable solutions for preserving dark night skies.</Callout>
## What happened
DarkSky International announced that the 2026 "Under One Sky" conference will take place entirely online on November 6‑7. The event is structured as a continuous 24‑hour program featuring keynote talks, panel discussions, and interactive workshops. Topics span astronomy, ecology, urban planning, and astrotourism, highlighting how light pollution affects both scientific research and ecosystems.
The organization highlighted a recent NASA night‑lights map showing pockets where artificial illumination is actually dimming, offering a hopeful data point for advocates. Participants will have access to this map, along with tools to assess local sky quality and strategies for community‑level lighting retrofits.
Registration is open to anyone interested, and the virtual format removes travel barriers, allowing contributors from dozens of countries to join live sessions or view recordings afterward.
## Why it matters
Light pollution erodes a shared cultural heritage, hampers astronomical discovery, and disrupts wildlife that rely on natural darkness. Approximately 99 % of people in the U.S. and Europe now live under polluted skies, and 80 % of North Americans cannot see the Milky Way. By convening experts and citizens, the conference seeks to accelerate policy changes, promote dark‑sky‑friendly lighting standards, and inspire grassroots action that can restore visibility for future generations.
The outcomes could influence municipal lighting codes, guide manufacturers toward shielded fixtures, and support conservation programs for nocturnal species. Moreover, a coordinated global effort can amplify the impact of localized successes, turning isolated wins into a broader movement.

## How to think about it
Treat the conference as a three‑step framework: (1) Diagnose your local sky using the NASA map or citizen‑science apps; (2) Learn from the workshop toolkits about shielded fixtures, curfews, and community outreach; (3) Implement a pilot project in your neighborhood or municipality and share results back with the DarkSky network. By iterating this loop, individuals can turn conference insights into measurable reductions in skyglow.
<Callout type="warning">Do not assume a single lighting solution works everywhere; local regulations, cultural attitudes, and ecosystem needs vary.</Callout>
