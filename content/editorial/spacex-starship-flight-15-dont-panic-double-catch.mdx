SpaceX is set to launch Starship Flight 15 from Starbase in Texas, emblazoned with the familiar “Don’t panic” slogan from Douglas Adams’s classic. The mission marks the 15th test flight and the first attempt to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the orbital stage with the launch tower’s “chopsticks” arms. In addition to the double‑catch challenge, the vehicle will release a second batch of 26 next‑gen Starlink V3 satellites. The cultural nod and technical milestone together signal how the company blends public imagination with reusable launch ambitions.
<Callout type="takeaway">SpaceX’s double‑catch test could prove fully reusable two‑stage launches are within reach.</Callout>
## What happened
On Nov 13, 2026, Starship Flight 15 lifted off during a 75‑minute window that opened at 5:45 p.m. EST. The Super Heavy first stage is slated to return to the launch pad for a chopsticks capture roughly 6.5 minutes after liftoff, while the upper stage, Ship 42, will continue to orbit.
Ship 42 will deploy 26 Starlink V3 satellites, the second set of that generation after the identical payload on Flight 14. After deployment, the spacecraft will circle Earth 14 times before attempting its own chopsticks catch on Nov 14, about 22.5 hours after launch.
The hull bears the phrase “Don’t panic,” a direct reference to The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy, where the answer to life, the universe and everything is famously 42—the same number as the vehicle’s serial designation.
## Why it matters
A successful double‑catch would demonstrate true two‑stage reusability, a breakthrough that could slash launch costs and accelerate the cadence of orbital missions. It also reinforces SpaceX’s strategy of turning ambitious engineering tests into public spectacles, shaping perception of space travel as both attainable and culturally resonant. Conversely, any failure would highlight the remaining technical hurdles before routine, rapid‑turnaround flights become a reality.

## How to think about it
Treat the flight as a data point rather than a definitive proof of operational capability. Watch the live stream, note the timing of each capture, and compare the outcome with previous single‑stage recoveries. Use the results to gauge how quickly reusable launch economics might improve, but keep an eye on subsequent flights that will validate durability and turnaround speed.
<Callout type="warning">A successful catch does not guarantee immediate operational use; future flights will still need extensive validation.</Callout>
