Astronomers using ALMA have captured an almost edge‑on view of the protoplanetary disk around the young star known as Gomez’s Hamburger (GoHam). The observations reveal distinct vertical layers of gas and dust and show the earliest stages of giant planet formation within the disk. Because the disk is tilted nearly edge‑on, scientists can directly map how material is arranged above and below the midplane, a perspective rarely available. This new window helps test theories about how massive planets can arise far from their host stars. Understanding these processes informs models of planetary system evolution across the galaxy.
<Callout type="takeaway">GoHam’s edge‑on orientation offers a unique laboratory for studying the vertical structure of giant planet birth.</Callout>
## What happened
ALMA observed millimeter‑wavelength emission from GoHam, mapping millimeter‑sized dust grains and several gas molecules (12CO, 13CO, CS, SO) in distinct layers. The lightest gas (12CO) sits highest above the midplane, 13CO lower, and CS closest to the midplane, confirming expected stratification. The disk’s large size, asymmetries, and detected winds suggest active processes that could seed giant planets at large orbital distances.
## Why it matters
The vertical stratification observed validates models that predict gas and dust settle at different heights, a key step toward core accretion in massive planets. It also shows that giant planets can begin forming far from the star, challenging the notion that they must migrate inward. Researchers can now benchmark simulations against a real system, improving predictions for exoplanet demographics.

## How to think about it
When evaluating new disk observations, compare vertical layering to model predictions, consider the role of winds and asymmetries, and treat edge‑on cases as complementary to more face‑on studies rather than universal templates.
<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that GoHam’s characteristics apply to all protoplanetary disks; its extreme size and orientation may make it an outlier.</Callout>
