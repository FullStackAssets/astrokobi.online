SpaceX has set a launch date for its most ambitious Starship test yet. Flight 15, scheduled for Friday, Nov 13, will try to catch both the Super Heavy booster and the Starship upper stage on the launch pad. The mission would mark the first complete recovery of an orbital launch system. Success would dramatically cut turnaround time and lower launch costs, while a failure could delay the Starlink megaconstellation rollout.

<Callout type="takeaway">Flight 15 aims to prove the first fully reusable orbital launch by catching both stages back on the launch mount.</Callout>

## What happened
SpaceX announced a 75‑minute launch window opening at 5:45 p.m. EST on Nov 13 from Starbase in South Texas. Super Heavy is expected to return to the pad about 6.5 minutes after liftoff for a tower “chopstick” catch, a maneuver already performed on three previous flights. The Starship upper stage will continue to orbit, deploy 26 next‑gen Starlink V3 satellites, then attempt a high‑energy re‑entry and land back on the mount for a second catch, a maneuver never tried before.

## Why it matters
A full‑stage catch would validate SpaceX’s vision of rapid, turn‑key launch operations, potentially shrinking the cost gap between low‑Earth‑orbit and deep‑space missions. Reusing both stages without refurbishment could accelerate the rollout of the next‑generation Starlink constellation, expanding global broadband coverage. Conversely, a missed catch would highlight the engineering challenges of high‑energy re‑entries and could push back timelines for both commercial and exploratory missions that depend on Starship’s promised cadence.

## How to think about it
Treat Flight 15 as a proof‑of‑concept milestone rather than a guaranteed production cadence. Stakeholders should monitor post‑flight data releases to gauge the reliability of the double‑catch sequence before committing to large‑scale contracts. For engineers, the test offers a concrete case study in high‑velocity aerodynamic braking and precision ground‑based capture, informing design choices for future reusable vehicles.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume immediate price reductions for satellite launches until multiple successful double‑catch flights are demonstrated.</Callout>
