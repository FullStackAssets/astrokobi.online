PBS’s new living documentary \"We Chose to Go to the Moon\" aired on Nov 10, 2026 at 8:00 p.m. ET on PBS, PBS.org, and the PBS app. It blends theater, concert, and documentary storytelling, using archival footage, historic artifacts, and live performances recorded at Carnegie Hall. The program revisits the global space race, the Apollo moon landings, and the Apollo 13 mission, featuring actors as astronauts and mathematicians. Broadway singers and the Orchestra of St. Luke’s perform period songs, creating an immersive experience. The event aims to engage space enthusiasts and general audiences with a fresh, multisensory narrative.

<Callout type="takeaway">The documentary shows that history can be relived through live performance and multimedia fusion.</Callout>

## What happened
The film premiered on PBS, PBS.org, and the PBS app at 8:00 p.m. ET on Nov 10, 2026. It is a filmed version of a live presentation staged at New York’s Carnegie Hall in April, produced by RadicalMedia.

Directed by Tony‑Award winner Michael Mayer and narrated by historian John Monsky, the production features actors portraying Neil Armstrong, Buzz Aldrin, Michael Collins, Ed White, Katherine Johnson, and Poppy Northcutt, among others. The cast includes Ian Weinberger, Skylar Astin, Brent Comer, Claybourne Elder, Elizabeth Gillies, Taylor Louderman, and Joy Woods.

A highlight is a tribute to Neil Armstrong performed by his son Mark and granddaughter Kali, singing \"Flight of Fancy\". The show also integrates period songs such as \"Fly Me to the Moon\", \"Rocket Man\", \"Space Oddity\", and \"Sound of Silence\", performed by Broadway vocalists and the Orchestra of St. Luke’s.

## Why it matters
By marrying archival material with live performance, the documentary makes the technical and political complexities of the 1960s space race accessible to a modern audience. It reinforces cultural memory of a pivotal era, inspiring renewed interest in STEM and space exploration. The format also demonstrates how public broadcasters can innovate storytelling to compete with on‑demand streaming.

## How to think about it
Treat the program as a hybrid educational tool: watch the narrative for historical context, then pause to explore the original archival clips and primary sources referenced. Use the musical interludes as entry points for discussions about the cultural impact of the space race. Pair viewing with classroom activities that compare dramatized portrayals to NASA’s official records.

<Callout type="warning">Do not rely on dramatized scenes as literal historical evidence without consulting primary sources.</Callout>
