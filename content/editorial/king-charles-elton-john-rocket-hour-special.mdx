On November 14, 2026, King Charles III sat down with Sir Elton John for a one‑hour episode of the Rocket Hour podcast on Apple Music. The special, recorded in Apple’s new Battersea studio, was timed to coincide with the 50th anniversary of The King’s Trust charity. Listeners heard the monarch and the music legend reminisce about iconic performances and spotlight artists who have benefited from the Trust’s grants. The collaboration highlights how cultural institutions and royalty can amplify philanthropic impact in the digital age.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Apple Music Rocket Hour special uses music and royalty to spotlight The King’s Trust’s five‑decade legacy.</Callout>

## What happened
King Charles arrived at Apple’s newly built Radio studio in London in early October, where he and Elton John recorded the hour‑long conversation for Rocket Hour. The episode features the pair sharing memories of landmark concerts, including Muse’s early grant‑supported equipment purchase and their 2018 benefit show at Royal Albert Hall.

In addition to the interview, the broadcast announced the launch of the Apple × King’s Trust Creative Labs, a series of workshops that give young creators access to technology, mentorship, and industry‑relevant skills.

The special aired on the king’s 78th birthday, positioning the program as both a celebration of the Trust’s history and a showcase of Apple’s commitment to cultural philanthropy.

## Why it matters
By pairing a globally recognized musician with a reigning monarch, Apple amplifies the visibility of The King’s Trust’s work, potentially attracting new donors and participants. The partnership also signals how legacy institutions can leverage streaming platforms to reach younger, digitally native audiences. For Apple, the event demonstrates the brand’s role beyond commerce, positioning it as a curator of cultural heritage.
