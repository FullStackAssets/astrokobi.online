NASA has opened the application window for its next astronaut class, targeting U.S. citizens who want to help build a sustainable lunar presence and eventually travel to Mars. The deadline to submit a complete application is 11:59 p.m. EST on Thursday, Dec 3, 2026. Candidates will be selected for training that emphasizes surface operations, long‑duration habitation, and the development of systems for the upcoming Lunar Gateway and a future lunar base. This call arrives as the United States prepares to return to the Moon and lay the groundwork for crewed missions to the Red Planet.

<Callout type="takeaway">NASA’s new astronaut class will be the first to train for sustained lunar presence and the path to Mars.</Callout>

## What happened
The agency announced the open call on its news‑release page, stating that the next generation of explorers will operate on the lunar surface, live and work off‑world, and push human exploration farther than ever before. Administrator Jared Isaacman emphasized that the selected astronauts could “step onto the Moon, live and work in the Lunar Base, and help develop the systems and operations that will ultimately take crews to Mars.”

NASA expects to make its final selection by the end of 2027, with training to begin shortly thereafter. The last recruitment cycle in 2024 attracted more than 8,000 applications, and in 2025 ten candidates were chosen and are now completing nearly two years of training. Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson reminded applicants that “the window of opportunity is open. The mission awaits.”

## Why it matters
Training a new cohort of astronauts for lunar surface operations directly supports the Artemis program’s goal of establishing a permanent human presence on the Moon. That foothold serves as a testbed for life‑support, habitat, and mobility technologies that will be essential for the first crewed missions to Mars. The selection also signals continued U.S. leadership in deep‑space exploration, influencing international partnerships, commercial investment, and the broader scientific agenda.

## How to think about it
If you are considering applying, start by confirming citizenship, degree, and experience requirements well before the deadline. Build a portfolio that highlights technical expertise, leadership, and problem‑solving in high‑stress environments. Seek out analog missions, flight‑training programs, or research projects that simulate off‑world conditions to strengthen your candidacy. Finally, treat the application as a long‑term career investment—prepare for a multi‑year commitment that may reshape your professional trajectory.

<Callout type="warning">Do not submit an incomplete application; missing documents will result in automatic disqualification.</Callout>
