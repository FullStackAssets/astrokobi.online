A crisp, sunny weekend is set for Houston, with daytime highs climbing into the mid‑70s. Sunday morning will be noticeably cooler, dipping into the upper 40s to low 50s. After the weekend, temperatures are expected to rise steadily into the low‑to‑mid 80s, with isolated highs near 90°F by Friday. Meanwhile, a developing system off the Carolinas could evolve into a nor'easter, bringing gusty winds and a 30% chance of tropical development. These contrasting conditions matter for outdoor plans and coastal weather watchers alike.

## What happened
Houston will enjoy clear skies and sunshine throughout Saturday and Sunday, with afternoon temperatures peaking in the mid‑70s. Sunday morning will feel chilly, with temperatures in the upper 40s to lower 50s, and a small craft risk remains for the evening due to light winds.

Looking ahead to next week, dry air will dominate and high temperatures will climb back into the low‑to‑mid 80s, with some locations possibly reaching 90°F by Friday. Rain chances stay below 10% each day, and dew points will be lower, keeping the air relatively dry.

A low‑pressure system is forming off the Carolinas late Sunday. While its exact track and intensity are uncertain, the National Hurricane Center notes a 30% chance it could develop tropical characteristics. The system may bring coastal flooding, high surf, and gusty winds from Florida through the Carolinas to New Jersey.

## Why it matters
The sunny, mild weekend offers ideal conditions for outdoor activities, but the chilly Sunday morning may require layered clothing. The upcoming warm spell provides a welcome break from recent cool weather, yet the low rain probability means limited natural watering for gardens. The potential nor'easter introduces a risk of sudden coastal impacts far from Houston, highlighting the interconnected nature of regional weather systems and the need for preparedness along the East Coast.
