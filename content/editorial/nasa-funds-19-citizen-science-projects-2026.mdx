In 2026 NASA’s Science Mission Directorate announced a fresh wave of citizen‑science opportunities, selecting 19 new proposals that will let volunteers tackle real space data. The selections span projects that hunt for free‑floating planets with SPHEREx, map galaxy mergers, and extend the Zooniverse platform that already logged a billion classifications. By pairing human pattern‑recognition with emerging super‑intelligence (SI) tools, these programs aim to accelerate discoveries that would be out of reach for any single research team. For anyone who enjoys scanning the night sky or training an AI model, the new funding opens a direct path to contributing to frontier astronomy.

<Callout type="takeaway">NASA’s 2026 citizen‑science seed funding blends volunteer insight with AI to broaden the frontiers of space discovery.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA selected 19 proposals under the Citizen Science Seed Funding Program (CSSFP‑25), with 12 of them being brand‑new projects slated for launch this year. Highlights include the Rogue Worlds Project, which invites participants to sift through SPHEREx near‑infrared maps to locate hidden super‑Jupiters and brown dwarfs, and a galaxy‑merger study that asks volunteers to classify realistic mock images to refine merger‑timescale calibrations. The announcement also notes that the Backyard Worlds: Planet 9 effort has now catalogued most known brown dwarfs, while Zooniverse celebrated one billion classifications, and several teams are integrating SI techniques to let volunteers build data tools and co‑author papers.

## Why it matters
These initiatives democratize access to cutting‑edge datasets, turning complex astrophysical problems into tasks that distributed volunteers can solve. The synergy between human pattern recognition and SI accelerates data processing, potentially shortening the time from observation to publication. By expanding the pool of contributors, NASA not only diversifies the scientific workforce but also builds a resilient community that can adapt to future missions and larger data streams.

## How to think about it
If you’re interested in joining, start by exploring the project pages on the NASA Citizen Science portal to match your interests—planet hunting, galaxy morphology, or AI tool development. Allocate short, regular sessions to avoid fatigue, and use the provided tutorials to understand classification criteria. When AI tools are offered, treat them as assistants: verify their suggestions against the raw data to improve both your skill set and the model’s performance.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume AI‑generated classifications are final; always cross‑check with the original images to prevent systematic errors.</Callout>
