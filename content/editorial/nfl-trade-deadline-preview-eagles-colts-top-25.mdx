The NFL trade deadline is just two weeks away, and the market is already heating up. Recent high‑profile moves like Joey Porter Jr. to the Cowboys and Marvin Harrison Jr. to the Texans have set the tone for a busy window. A new ranking highlights 25 players who could be on the move, with the Philadelphia Eagles and Indianapolis Colts supplying the bulk of the list. Understanding which assets are most likely to change hands helps fans and analysts anticipate how the second half of the season will unfold.

<Callout type="takeaway">The Eagles and Colts account for the majority of the trade deadline’s most coveted assets.</Callout>

## What happened
The CBS Sports ranking enumerates 25 potential trade targets, noting four Eagles players, three Colts players, and two starting quarterbacks among the most watched. Saquon Barkley’s contract is complicated by option bonuses, yet his age and the Eagles’ struggles keep him on the radar despite a recent hamstring pull. Defensive end Moro Ojomo, entering the final year of his rookie deal, is a candidate as the Eagles have already invested heavily in Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis.

The Eagles could also move Nolan Smith, whose fifth‑year option was exercised, and tight end Dallas Goedert, who now carries a modest $1.3 million base salary after renegotiation. On the Colts side, running back Jonathan Taylor remains productive at 4.2 yards per carry even as the offense falters, making him an attractive piece for a contender.

## Why it matters
These moves could reshape the competitive balance in the NFC and AFC, giving struggling teams a chance to rebuild while allowing playoff hopefuls to add proven talent. Salary‑cap considerations, injury histories, and contract structures will dictate which deals materialize, influencing roster depth and on‑field performance for the remainder of the season.

## How to think about it
Evaluate each candidate through three lenses: (1) contract flexibility, (2) injury risk, and (3) positional need for likely suitors. Prioritize players whose salaries align with a team’s cap space and whose health status is clear. Consider the broader strategic context—whether a franchise is rebuilding or pushing for a playoff run—before assuming a trade will happen.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume a trade will happen solely because a player appears on the list; front‑office strategy and salary‑cap constraints often override speculation.</Callout>
