The Planetary Society has launched its annual Best of 2026 Awards, inviting members to vote on the year’s most impactful space science missions and Society achievements. Voting runs through November 30, with results expected in early December. This open call lets enthusiasts highlight the work that resonated most with the community. By surfacing favorite projects, the awards help signal where public enthusiasm and support lie, which can influence future outreach and funding priorities.

<Callout type="takeaway">Community voting reveals what space enthusiasts value most in a given year.</Callout>

## What happened
The Society announced a multi‑category voting process that includes "Best performance by a space science mission" and "Best Planetary Society accomplishment." Members can also suggest additional favorites in the Society’s online community forum. Voting closes on November 30, and an email will deliver the results to participants in early December.

## Why it matters
When members collectively endorse particular missions or achievements, it creates a grassroots barometer of public interest. That feedback can guide the Society’s advocacy, outreach focus, and even inform policymakers about which projects enjoy broad support. It also strengthens the sense of ownership among hobbyists, professionals, and students who see their preferences reflected in a formal recognition.

## How to think about it
Treat the awards as a snapshot of community sentiment rather than a definitive ranking of scientific value. Use the results to explore why certain missions captured imaginations—look at their discoveries, media coverage, and educational outreach. When discussing the outcomes, balance enthusiasm with critical assessment of each project's long‑term impact.

<Callout type="warning">Do not assume that winning an award guarantees increased funding or policy priority for the highlighted mission.</Callout>
