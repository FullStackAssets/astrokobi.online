On December 5, Nebula’s travel‑competition series “Jet Lag: The Game” will debut its 20th season, “Hide + Seek: Italy,” on more than 160 cinema screens across the United States, United Kingdom and Canada. The 90‑minute premiere is being rolled out in partnership with Regal, Picturehouse, Cineworld and Landmark Cinemas. This marks the series’ biggest theatrical rollout yet, expanding a format that has traditionally lived on streaming platforms. The move signals a new hybrid distribution model for niche original content.
<Callout type="takeaway">The theatrical launch turns a streaming‑only series into a shared‑screen event, testing demand for event‑style TV releases.</Callout>
## What happened
Nebula announced that tickets for the “Hide + Seek: Italy” premiere go on sale Nov 5, with screenings scheduled for Dec 5 in over 160 locations across the three countries. The partnership with major cinema chains mirrors the Season 19 “Japanorama” rollout, which was the series’ first international theater screening in August and October.
Season 20 raises the stakes by introducing an “Expert Mode” that removes the most powerful questions and adds penalties for seekers, according to Nebula’s description. The show’s creators Sam Denby, Ben Doyle and Adam Chase will lead the competition, joined by a rotating cast of guest competitors.
## Why it matters
By moving a niche streaming series into cinemas, Nebula is probing whether fans will pay premium ticket prices for a limited‑time, communal viewing experience, potentially reshaping revenue models for independent content producers. Success could encourage other platforms to stage theatrical events for flagship shows, while failure would reaffirm the dominance of on‑demand streaming.

## How to think about it
Treat the theatrical premiere as a test case: monitor ticket sales, social media chatter, and post‑event subscriber growth to gauge whether event‑style releases can complement a streaming catalog. If the data shows a net lift, consider scaling similar events for other high‑profile series; if not, focus on strengthening the core on‑demand offering.
<Callout type="warning">Do not assume the cinema screening replaces the regular streaming release; the episodes will still debut on Nebula after the theatrical window.</Callout>
