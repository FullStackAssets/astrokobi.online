NASA has opened its astronaut application window for the next class, targeting missions to the lunar surface, a permanent Moon Base, and eventual crewed flights to Mars. The deadline is 11:59 p.m. EST on Dec 3, 2026, and only U.S. citizens may apply. This recruitment follows a 2024 call that yielded over 8,000 applicants and a 2025 class now in training. The new cohort will be the first to train specifically for sustained lunar operations and the groundwork for Mars, shaping the next era of human spaceflight.

<Callout type="takeaway">NASA’s upcoming astronaut class will be the first trained for long‑duration lunar base work and the path to crewed Mars missions.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA announced on Nov 7 2026 that applications are open for its next astronaut class, with a deadline of Dec 3 2026. The agency emphasizes that candidates will support the Artemis program’s lunar surface activities, the construction of a Moon Base, and the development of systems for future Mars missions.

The selection process will culminate in late 2027, when NASA expects to announce the new candidates who will begin training the following year. The previous 2025 class was chosen from over 8,000 applicants and is currently completing a two‑year training pipeline.

NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman and Deputy Administrator Matt Anderson highlighted the historic nature of the opportunity, noting that the chosen explorers will help write the next chapter of humanity’s presence beyond Earth.

## Why it matters
Training astronauts specifically for long‑duration lunar habitation accelerates the technical readiness of habitats, life‑support, and surface operations that are prerequisites for sustainable Mars exploration. A dedicated lunar workforce also strengthens U.S. leadership in deep‑space activities, attracting commercial partners and inspiring the next generation of scientists and engineers. Conversely, the success of these missions will hinge on how quickly the new class can translate training into operational capability, influencing budget allocations and international collaboration.

## How to think about it
If you are considering applying, start by mapping your experience to the core competencies NASA cites: technical excellence, teamwork, and adaptability in extreme environments. Build a portfolio that showcases multidisciplinary skills—engineering, science, or operational leadership—and seek out analog missions or simulations that mimic lunar conditions. Treat the application as a long‑term career commitment; the selection timeline extends beyond 2027, and training will span several years before any flight assignment.

<Callout type="warning">Only complete, U.S.-citizen applications submitted by the Dec 3 deadline will be considered; incomplete or late submissions are automatically disqualified.</Callout>
