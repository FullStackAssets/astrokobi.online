The National Aeronautics and Space Administration has opened its next astronaut‑selection window, inviting U.S. citizens to apply by 11:59 p.m. EST on Dec 3, 2026. The agency is seeking explorers who will train for lunar surface operations, help build the forthcoming Moon Base, and eventually support crewed missions to Mars. Over 8,000 applicants responded to the 2024 call, and NASA expects to choose a new class that will begin training in late 2027. This recruitment marks a pivotal step as America returns to the Moon and prepares for humanity’s first interplanetary voyages.

<Callout type="takeaway">NASA’s new astronaut class will be the first to train for sustained lunar presence and future Mars exploration.</Callout>

## What happened
NASA announced that applications for the next astronaut class are now open, with a deadline of 11:59 p.m. EST on Thursday, Dec 3, 2026. Candidates must be U.S. citizens and submit a complete set of materials through the agency’s online portal.

The selection process follows the 2024 round, which attracted more than 8,000 applicants and produced ten astronaut candidates in 2025. Those ten are currently completing nearly two years of training, and the new cohort will be selected in late 2027 to begin a similar training pipeline.

The Astronaut Selection Board, chaired by chief astronaut Scott Tingle, will evaluate applicants on technical expertise, judgment, teamwork, and courage, aiming to staff the crews that will operate on the Moon Base and eventually travel to Mars.

## Why it matters
The new class will directly support NASA’s Artemis‑derived lunar infrastructure and the long‑term goal of establishing a permanent human presence on the Moon. By training astronauts now, the agency shortens the timeline for crewed Mars missions, which rely on lessons learned from sustained lunar operations. The selection also signals to the broader scientific and engineering community that space exploration careers are expanding, potentially inspiring a new generation of talent.

## How to think about it
If you are considering applying, start by mapping your existing qualifications—advanced STEM degrees, flight experience, or operational expertise—to the competencies NASA emphasizes. Build a portfolio that showcases problem‑solving under pressure and teamwork in high‑risk environments. Use the official application checklist to ensure every required document is included, and seek feedback from mentors who understand the selection criteria before you submit.

<Callout type="warning">Do not submit an incomplete application after the deadline; incomplete or late submissions will be automatically disqualified.</Callout>
