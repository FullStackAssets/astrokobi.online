The Tiangong space station is set to welcome its first non‑Chinese crew member next month. On 25 November 2026, China will launch Shenzhou 24, carrying two taikonauts and a Pakistani astronaut selected by SUPARCO. The astronaut will serve as a payload specialist for a week‑long stay before returning on Shenzhou‑23. This milestone marks a new era of international crewed flights from China.

<Callout type="takeaway">The first foreign astronaut on Tiangong signals China's move toward broader international crewed missions.</Callout>

## What happened
China’s 18th crewed flight, Shenzhou 24, will lift off aboard a Long March 2F rocket on 25 November 2026. The three‑person crew will dock with the Tianhe core module of the Tiangong station, where the Pakistani astronaut will join the pilot and commander for a seven‑day expedition.

The partnership began with a bilateral agreement signed in February 2025, committing China to train Pakistani candidates. After a rigorous selection process that concluded in April 2026, Muhammad Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud were nominated, with one slated to fly and the other serving as backup.

## Why it matters
For China, the flight demonstrates a willingness to open its crewed program to foreign partners, aligning its approach with NASA and ESA and bolstering its credibility ahead of the International Lunar Research Station. Pakistan gains a rapid entry onto the crewed‑flight stage, narrowing its gap with India’s Gaganyaan timeline and enhancing its scientific and diplomatic standing. The mission also reshapes the geopolitical landscape of low‑Earth‑orbit cooperation, hinting at future multilateral projects that could extend beyond bilateral China‑Pakistan ties.

## How to think about it
Treat the flight as a diplomatic lever as much as a scientific venture. Track subsequent agreements for joint experiments, technology sharing, and crew‑exchange protocols. Assess how the partnership influences regional space policy, especially India’s crewed‑flight schedule, and watch for any follow‑on missions that expand the roster of non‑Chinese participants.
