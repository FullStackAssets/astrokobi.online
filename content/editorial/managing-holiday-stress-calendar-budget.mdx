Holiday seasons can turn a brief break into a marathon of obligations, amplifying stress for many. NASA’s MAF EAP team recently outlined a step‑by‑step approach to keep the "Bah Humbug" at bay. The guidance emphasizes tracking commitments, budgeting for hidden costs, and preserving personal routines. By treating the holiday schedule like any mission plan, individuals can reduce friction and protect wellbeing.
<Callout type="takeaway">A clear calendar and realistic budget are the most effective antidotes to holiday stress.</Callout>
## What happened
The article notes that the first step is to acknowledge personal feelings about the holidays, allowing each preference—whether decorating, gift‑shopping, or opting out—to be valid. It then advises compiling all expected events into a single calendar, tagging each with the required contribution (dish, gift, visit) and checking availability before committing. A parallel budget sheet captures gifts, extra groceries, travel, utilities and other incidental expenses, with receipts tracked and family members informed of limits.
## Why it matters
Unmanaged holiday overload can erode mental health, strain relationships, and cause financial overspend, affecting families and workplaces. Recognizing the issue early lets people set boundaries, maintain sleep and nutrition, and keep productivity.

## How to think about it
Identify your emotional stance, list every invitation with required roles, allocate time and money in separate columns, review the plan weekly, and adjust for new information while preserving core self‑care blocks.
<Callout type="warning">Do not let the schedule become a source of anxiety; allow flexibility for unexpected changes.</Callout>
